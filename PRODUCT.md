# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: media-literate readers who want to understand how their own news diet compares to the global sentiment baseline — where their information sources sit relative to the wider landscape, and how far that is from consensus. They arrive skeptical of being told what to think and want evidence, not verdicts.

Secondary: journalists and researchers studying cross-source narrative divergence — how the same entity (a leader, a country, an event) is portrayed differently across outlets and countries over time.

## Product Purpose

A computational, non-judgmental framework for analyzing global news sentiment patterns. It extracts entity-sentiment data from news articles worldwide and visualizes how different sources and countries position the same entities (people, states, organizations) along power and moral axes over time. Success looks like a reader leaving with a concrete, non-partisan picture of where their news sources diverge from the global average — not a label telling them who's "biased."

## Positioning

The mirror that shows you the information environment you inhabit — not the verdict on it.

## Brand Personality

Rigorous, revealing, unbiased. A newsroom-wire-service register: editorial confidence and restraint rather than SaaS-dashboard chrome. Calm and evidentiary in tone — it presents patterns and lets them speak, never accusatory or alarmist ("propaganda detector" framing is explicitly avoided).

## Anti-references

- Generic red/blue left-right bias charts (AllSides, Ad Fontes Media style spectrums) — the project explicitly rejects simplistic, American-centric political spectrums.
- The current site's own look: default MUI cards, generic dashboard chrome, gradient hero-metric templates — this is the thing being replaced.
- Tabloid "gotcha" framing that names sources as biased/propagandist. The tool reveals divergence; it does not accuse.

## Design Principles

- **Reveal, don't judge.** Every visual choice makes divergence legible without implying a "correct" side.
- **Global sentiment as the reference line.** The worldwide baseline stays visible so a source's position reads as distance from consensus, not distance from "right."
- **Escape the bubble.** A reader should be able to locate their own information diet within the broader landscape at a glance.
- **Beyond left-right.** Never use red/blue, party colors, or any single-country political spectrum as a layout or color metaphor.
- **Newsroom credibility.** Editorial typography and restraint over dashboard chrome — it should read like a serious wire-service instrument, not a SaaS analytics product.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. No additional accessibility requirements specified; standard color-contrast, keyboard-navigation, and reduced-motion support apply.
